I recently had the chance to finish *Black Myth: Wukong*, and to say the least, the game is a historical masterpiece.
Developed entirely in China, *Black Myth* was released in 2024 and was one of the top contenders for Game of the Year—and after playing it, I understood why.

Through my experience, I found myself immersed in a work of art that tells the story—or rather, the legend—of Wukong. And who doesn’t know him? The game also stands out for its dynamic and fluid combat, which makes for a fast-paced experience, in addition to its beautiful skills and mechanics, most notably the character’s transformations and use of magic. and let’s not forget the graphics, which meet world-class standards.

Despite all the positives, I did encounter some difficulties and issues, including camera problems during certain boss fights, difficulty locating some areas on the map, and—most importantly—technical glitches, especially since the game was developed using the Unreal Engine 5.

In short, Black Myth: Wukong remains one of the most notable Chinese contributions to the gaming industry and a strong competitor in the face of American games dominating the market. So, for those who haven’t tried it yet, I recommend giving it a shot despite its difficulty.
